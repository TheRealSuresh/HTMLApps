06 · WHAT'S IN THE SCORES

17 things that can wear an economy down

Each country is judged on three questions:

  1. How much damage is building at home? The 17 factors on the right, each scored 1 to 10. Factors that can do harm fastest count most. One very bad score can't be averaged away, and a borrowing boom that feeds a bubble adds a penalty.
  2. How much money does it have to absorb a shock? Reserves, sovereign wealth funds and room to borrow, cut by 40% on the part held in markets that could crash at the same time. This can lower the score by up to 25%.
  3. How much trouble does it import? Problems spill over from trade partners in difficulty and, for euro members, from each other.

Final verdict: 7.0 or more erodes first; 5.5 to 6.99 at risk; 4.0 to 5.49 muddles through; below 4.0 succeeds.

Scores as of 18 Oct 2026, from IMF, World Bank, BIS and other published data plus the author's judgement. A hobby model built with the help of AI. Not investment advice.

"Count stockpiles and pension funds" adds government oil, grain and other strategic stockpiles at full value, and 25% of public pension and social-security reserve funds (the rest is owed to retirees), with the same 40% crash cut on the half held in shares. It raises the cushion by up to 2 points.

Dollar cushion sizes are approximate 2025-26 estimates of FX reserves plus major sovereign wealth funds. Russia counts only reserves it can still reach. Argentina is gross reserves (its net reserves are negative). The United States holds a small cushion but issues the world's main reserve currency, which the model credits separately.
